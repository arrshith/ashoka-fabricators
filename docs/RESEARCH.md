## Design research and chosen direction
Reviewed https://dribbble.com/tags/web-design-inspiration and https://www.awwwards.com/websites/web-design/; discovered industrial/architectural examples HARUCHIKA Manufacturing, Summit Steel Works and Path Robotics. Competitor research covered Hörmann and Ditec product categorization. Principles: clear product taxonomy, large imagery, disciplined type, purposeful motion, fast enquiry access. No source branding, assets or layouts copied.

Concept: engineered entrances. Architectural product publication with a steel-dark photographic opening, authentic red logo, light reading surfaces and generous typography. Palette: #202627, #131819, #f4f4ee, #ffffff, #b9202e, #a4a8a2. Left alignment; broad photo panels; restrained reveal interaction. Mobile uses vertical flow and a compact quote bar. No heavy WebGL required.

Architecture: Home → product collection → product detail → prefilled quote. Preliminary solution finder uses application and operation; dimensions never generate engineering specifications. About, honest project empty state, resources, contact, privacy and terms complete the MVP.

### Why the scale finding changed the design

The original brief asked whether the site "communicates scale". For a
ten-person, thousand-square-foot workshop, it deliberately does not. Performing
the scale of a multinational door manufacturer would be untrue and would ring
false to anyone who has visited. The site targets a premium of **precision and
longevity** instead — fifty-four years, one workshop, made to the opening.
This is recorded as a considered deviation from the brief, not an oversight.
See `docs/DIRECTION.md`.
